\section{Simulation design}\label{sec:design}

The simulation uses a generator with known $\theta$ and varies one factor at a time around a base scenario, so that each theoretical result has a scenario that tests it against a prior numerical prediction.

\paragraph{Generator.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Covariates:} $V=(V_1,\dots,V_4)$ normal with correlations $0.3^{|j-k|}$, plus a three-level categorical $V_5$ whose distribution depends on $V_1$; all are customer characteristics. An unobserved $U$, independent of $V$, enters only the unobserved-confounding scenario.
  \item \emph{Latent channel variable:} $H=\ind\{C^*\ne\vacio\}$ indicates an actual history, with $\PP(H=1\mid V)=\sigma(-0.4+0.8V_1+0.3V_3)$; if $H=1$, the history takes the value $N(0.5V_1,1)$. It is recorded if $D=1$ and $H=1$, and $C=\vacio$ otherwise.
  \item \emph{Channel:} $\PP(D=1\mid V,H,U)=\sigma(\alpha_0+\omega\,\alpha^\top V+\zeta H+\nu U)$. The parameter $\omega$ controls overlap, and $\zeta\ne0$ makes the distribution of $C^*$ given $V$ differ between channels, a necessary condition for $C^*$ to confound.
  \item \emph{Outcomes:} two common products, $\PP(Y_k(d)=1\mid X^*,U)=\sigma(\beta_{k0}+\gamma_k d+\beta_k^\top V+\delta H+\nu'U+b)$ for $k=1,2$, with $\sigma$ the logistic function and $b$ a customer random effect, and a product exclusive to the premium channel with $Y_3(0)=0$. The outcome used by the estimators is $Y=Y_1+Y_2$.
  \item \emph{Target group:} $S=\ind\{D=1,\ s^*(V)\le c^*\}$, with $s^*$ the true purchase-propensity index in the premium channel and $c^*$ its 10th percentile in that channel. The rule is fixed, so $\theta$ does not change across replications.
  \item \emph{Units and observations:} each customer generates between 1 and 5 calls, a number drawn independently of all other variables, with channel and covariates constant across them; the unit- and observation-level estimands therefore coincide.
  \item \emph{Truth:} $\theta$ and the theoretical limit of each estimator are computed on a population of 1.5 million customers; the true conditional means and $dP_V/dQ_V$ are obtained by integrating out $H$, $U$ and the customer effect with Gauss--Hermite quadrature. Their Monte Carlo error, about 0.001 points (0.006 under confounding through the history and 0.015 under unobserved confounding), is negligible relative to that of the replication means. Trimming effects, much smaller, are predicted as paired differences within the population.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Scenarios and predictions.} The base scenario excludes $C$, has $\zeta\ne0$ and $\delta=\nu=\nu'=0$, high overlap ($\omega=0.5$; $\chi^2=3.6$), LightGBM \citep{ke2017} with five-fold cross-fitting by customer for $\hat p$ and $\hat m$, and 30{,}000 customers per replication: about 62{,}000 donor calls and 2{,}800 target-group calls. Around it we vary the inclusion of $C$, confounding through the history ($\delta=1.2$), overlap ($\omega=1.5$ and $3$; $\chi^2=6.3$ and about 36; the latter is unstable because $w^2$ is very heavy-tailed: on six populations of 1.5 million customers it ranged from 33 to 63, so the theoretical $\neff/n_0$ under low overlap, 0.03, is only indicative), model specification, unobserved confounding ($\nu=\nu'=1$), trimming at the 99th, 95th and 90th percentiles of the donor weights, and size (3{,}000 to 30{,}000 customers). Each scenario has 200 replications (the bootstrap and trimming sign-test runs have fewer; Section~\ref{sec:results}), so that 95\,\% coverage is estimated with a standard error of about 1.5 points. Predictions that do not follow directly from the theory were registered, with a date, before running the corresponding scenario; the code, the predictions with their commit timestamps, and the raw results are available at \url{REPOSITORIO}. Registration here means a time-stamped commit in that repository before the run, not a third-party registry.

\paragraph{The AUC does not measure overlap.} When setting $\omega$, the AUC of the ideal classifier stayed between 0.89 and 0.93 for $\omega$ between 0.3 and 2, while $\chi^2$ ranged from 3.5 to about 11, not monotonically (it is smallest for $\omega$ between 0.5 and 1). The definition of the target group, a cutoff on a score, already separates it from the donor channel. We therefore set the levels by $\chi^2$, which is what governs the variance (Proposition~\ref{prop:variance}).
